# Architecture and integration

## Atmospheric fog from Volumetric Fog and Rain

To fade distant mist into weather haze, enable **Share Atmosphere With Ground Fog** on the Volumetric Rain renderer feature for the same camera. The ground renderer uses atmospheric light and transmission at each contributing raymarch sample. Wisps close to a clear camera retain their detail, and an intervening fog bank reduces contrast in more distant wisps according to its density. The volume also respects the weather camera's exclusions.

The field is camera-specific and lasts for one render. Full, half and quarter resolution use the same lighting calculation, including the full-resolution fallback at geometry edges. A camera without a shared atmosphere uses the ordinary Interactive Fog rendering path.

## Simulation and rendering

`FogVolume` owns its GPU resources; it advances once in `LateUpdate`, independently of cameras. Disabling or destroying it releases every texture and the private compute-shader instance. Edit mode displays initialized fog without advancing time. `ResetSimulation()` restores the seeded density field; `Initialize()` reallocates after resolution or size changes. For external clocks, disable `automaticSimulation`, sample actor motion with `FogInteractor.SampleMotion(dt)`, and call `Advance(dt)`.

The default Volume3D velocity solver performs clamped MacCormack velocity advection, vorticity confinement, wind relaxation, interaction forces, Jacobi pressure iterations, and pressure-gradient subtraction. A collocated finite-difference grid is used; this is an approximate projection rather than a staggered MAC solver. Walls cancel normal velocity. Density uses conservative face fluxes with monotonized-central reconstruction and a shared donor budget. Each cell receives exactly the density its neighbors lose, and outgoing transfers cannot overdraw a donor. Large velocities remain positive but become more diffusive; this is not an unlimited-CFL accuracy guarantee. Optional replenishment supplies density only along the outer floor rim. Interior density also optionally relaxes toward a cached, unmasked resting field. This Rest Recovery term adds/removes density; strict transport conservation requires Rest Recovery, replenishment, and interactor clearing all to be zero. The cached pattern is rebuilt on initialization/reset and when Fog Height changes. There is no hot-smoke buoyancy; lifted fog settles gently. Initialization fills connected, full-height billows with a softly tapered density profile. Sparse local coordinate deformation bends their contours while retaining their three-dimensional body. Broad horizontal stream-function circulation persists under the wakes. Additional vertical damping keeps that circulation shallow without deleting density.

Characters are vertical capsules swept from their previous position. Velocity entrainment and a broad counter-rotating vortex pair extend beyond the body. Radial push and upward lift are deliberately weaker than the horizontal rolling motion. Density clearing is opt-in and defaults to zero. A stationary default interactor neither removes density nor damps passing flow. Use additional small interactors on animated feet when their motion matters. Motion over three metres in one sample is treated as a teleport. A gust injects velocity for a finite duration; zero requested velocity produces an outward blast. `AddSweep(from, to, velocity, radius, duration)` injects a swept spherical force for blades or moving props. Force and velocity caps keep authoring errors bounded.

The renderer intersects each view ray with the field and stops at opaque scene depth. Beer–Lambert transmittance accumulates front-to-back with early exit. A separate lighting grid stores directional transmittance and point-light irradiance. Two advected coordinate fields carry fine detail with the flow. Their fourth channel transports a wake-activity marker injected by moving interactors and forces; it decays after they pass. Two scales of analytic curl stirring, modulated by that marker, feed the velocity solver before projection. This is art-directed turbulence, not an energy-resolved turbulence model. Staggered six-second resets use exact integer simulation ticks and limit stretching; the resetting field has zero blend weight. Density and detail coordinates interpolate between the two latest simulation states, introducing one simulation tick of visual latency. The shader uses static pixel jitter; no screen-space history, motion vectors, or TAA dependency is required. Lighting still updates at the simulation rate. A 512 x 512 RGHalf texture caches a horizontal displacement field once per allocation. Sparse compact-support turns have jittered centres, radii from 1.1 to 2.2 coordinate units, varied angles, and both handednesses. Displacement vanishes smoothly outside each support. The cache bends the original three-dimensional billow noise, both during density initialization and through the advected render coordinate maps. It stores coordinate offsets. The pattern repeats every 16 coordinate units (24 metres at the default Fold Size); it is art-directed local deformation rather than a dynamically generated vortex simulation. Render-only curl displacement further shapes the contours, with high-frequency detail kept subordinate. Noise changes ridge contrast without zeroing the lower layer; erosion is restricted to the thin fringe. This does not modify stored density: rendered opacity is not a mass-conservation measurement. Displacement fades near the floor to avoid a detached sheet. The default full-resolution composite executes before transparents and post-processing. It preserves the camera target's sample count.

## Planar2D backend

`FogPlanarSimulation` owns independent 2D textures over X/Z. Column mass uses conservative face fluxes with monotonized-central reconstruction and a shared donor budget. Velocity uses semi-Lagrangian advection, 2D curl confinement, interaction forces and a two-axis Jacobi pressure solve. Density recovery and the transported wake marker use the same rate and gating thresholds as Volume3D. Projection is approximate; recovery, replenishment and clearing remain explicit mass sources/sinks.

A static RFloat volume retains the billowy resting shape. At rendering time its density is modulated by the interpolated transported/resting column-mass ratio, capped at three for visual stability. Detail coordinates move horizontally with the fluid and retain the sample's vertical coordinate. Consequently rendered opacity is not a direct measure of conserved column mass. A separate lighting volume has half as many X/Z samples as the fluid grid and the configured Y samples; it evaluates directional and point-light attenuation through reconstructed density.

Obstacles block columns where they intersect the lower bank's representative height (30% of Fog Height). Overhangs, vertical circulation, and motion confined to different heights cannot be represented independently. Elevated sources retain a vertical influence falloff but inject horizontal forces only. Use Volume3D where these differences matter.

`Density`, `Velocity`, `SolidMask` and `DetailCoordinates` expose 2D textures in Planar2D, versus 3D textures in Volume3D. Planar `Density` contains integrated column mass, not density at a height. Backend changes restart the field. Height changes preserve live column mass and velocity, update the shape cache immediately and rebuild the mass-recovery target.

## Reduced-resolution rendering

Full resolution uses one composite pass. Half and Quarter integrate fog into an RGBAHalf radiance/transmittance texture and RFloat depth texture, then apply a depth-aware reconstruction pass at full scene resolution. The scene color is sampled only at full resolution. Four neighboring low-resolution fog samples are weighted by bilinear position and eye-depth agreement. When their combined weight is insufficient, the full-resolution pixel is integrated directly. This avoids borrowing fog from an incompatible depth at thin geometry, at additional cost.

There is no temporal accumulation or reconstruction history. Both perspective and orthographic depth are handled. Frustum culling skips only the rendering of volumes outside the camera; simulation continues independently.

## Style profiles

`FogVolume.style` optionally references a `FogStyleProfile`. `ResolvedStyle` reads its settings without mutating either the asset or the preserved inline fields. Simulation backend, render resolution, grid/bounds, simulation rate, pressure iterations, ray steps and scene light references stay on the volume. Clearing Style returns to inline tuning. In Volume3D, a height change in Play Mode rebuilds only the resting-density target, preserving live flow and density; recovery gradually approaches the new target. Planar2D additionally updates its reconstructed shape immediately, as described above. Edit Mode resets the preview for a height change. See [Performance and style profiles](Performance-and-Styles.md) for authoring and cost controls.

## Tuning

- **Rest Recovery:** default 0.25/s. A cached resting density restores quiet ridges and coverage exponentially, including removing excess lifted density. The transported wake marker suppresses recovery while activity is above 0.2, smoothly restoring it between 0.2 and 0.02. Current sources participate immediately, so the first disturbance tick is protected too. Once fully quiet, 0.25/s closes half the remaining density gap in about 2.8 seconds; ongoing flow and wake decay make visible recovery slower. Zero disables this artistic density source/sink. Quiet velocity also eases toward the existing wind plus broad horizontal circulation at twice the density recovery rate. This prevents lingering energetic motion from shredding recovered ridges; velocity is never reset in one frame. Previously blocked space can recover after an obstacle moves because the cached field is unmasked; the current solid mask still wins.
- **Fold Size:** a render-only spatial multiplier, default 1.5. Larger values produce broader curved bands independently of the physical Eddy Size. It scales noise coordinates, not the simulation grid or displacement amplitude.

- **Interactor drag:** zero stops new force and wake-activity injection from that interactor. Supplemental vortex/activity strength fades between drag zero and one; direct velocity entrainment continues to respond across the full drag range. Child interactors, explicit gusts/sweeps, and density clearing remain independent. Existing fluid motion is not erased when drag becomes zero.
- **Grid:** default 128 × 24 × 128 across 16 × 2 × 16 m gives 12.5 cm horizontal and 8.3 cm vertical cells. Increasing each axis by two increases voxel work and memory by eight.
- **Fog height / extinction:** control bank depth and visibility separately. A tall uniform opaque layer hides wakes; preserve shallow troughs between ridges without breaking the lower layer into islands.
- **Turbulence / eddy size:** control motion-driven stirring and its scale. The activity marker decays with a roughly 1.1-second half-life; a small ambient term remains. Excess strength obscures the large rolling structures.
- **Wisp displacement / detail:** control render-only contour deformation in metres and fold contrast/edge erosion. Zero displacement disables contour warping. Keep both modest enough to preserve connected banks.
- **Vorticity:** strengthens existing curls. Excess confinement looks agitated and can amplify grid artifacts.
- **Replenishment:** controls supply at the field perimeter, not local wake recovery. Zero disables all replenishment. Settling, advection, and velocity damping control how a wake closes.
- **Pressure iterations:** trade projection accuracy against GPU time. More iterations matter for wide disturbances.
- **Ray steps:** primarily affects diagonal and grazing views. This cost scales with screen pixels; grid cost does not.
- **Lighting:** a grazing directional key and a dim ambient fill reveal billows. Assign point lights explicitly when lanterns should scatter into the fog.

With Ground Mist, the sample uses 160 × 32 × 160 cells (10 cm horizontal, 6.25 cm vertical), 112 ray steps, and 82.25 MiB of textures. The default grid allocates approximately 40 MiB of textures (104 bytes per cell plus a fixed 1 MiB pattern cache), excluding driver overhead and camera render targets. The solver is intentionally bounded to a room rather than a world-sized field. Render Graph composites currently reference the live volume resources; do not resize or destroy volumes from render callbacks.

## Cumulus morphology

The optional Cumulus shape builds irregularly sized clusters with rounded cores, smaller attached lobes, cellular density variation and tapered curled ribbons. Cloud Coverage adds a second population of overlapping, irregularly sized rounded billows between the main clusters. A short curl deformation is baked into the attached fringe while preserving rounded cores. The fluid transports a smooth density proxy; a higher-resolution cache supplies the finer rendered density and unsigned filament distance and taper opacity. Both detail-coordinate maps sample the cache, and each ribbon's coverage and fine-density/proxy ratio are evaluated before the maps are blended. Normalizing the maps separately preserves the blend weight when the other map samples empty space. Lighting advances to the completed simulation tick before evaluating these maps. This separates small rendered features from the coarser fluid grid without adding fluid cells.

Cumulus lighting samples the detailed density and approximates diffuse scattering with a broader attenuation lobe, reusing the existing light march. Solid obstacle occlusion is retained. A density-dependent lighting response gives dense cores more definition while keeping the wisps translucent. Its Plume Lift adds bounded vertical circulation only to Volume3D; Planar2D still reconstructs a static vertical profile from transported column mass. The common wake-gated recovery restores the selected morphology after interaction. Changing Density Shape, Puff Size, Cloud Coverage or Fog Height rebuilds the recovery target and detail cache without resetting the live fluid state. See [style controls and memory costs](Performance-and-Styles.md).

## Research

- [Bridson, Hourihan, Nordenstam, Curl-Noise for Procedural Fluid Flow](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph2007-curlnoise.pdf): curl fields and procedural wake detail. This package uses analytic curl patterns and a separate approximate projection, not the paper's exact boundary-constrained noise construction.

- [Harris, Fast Fluid Dynamics Simulation on the GPU](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-vi-beyond-triangles/chapter-38-fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-gpu): grid advection and pressure projection.
- [Crane, Llamas, Tariq, Real-Time Simulation and Rendering of 3D Fluids](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-v-physics-simulation/chapter-30-real-time-simulation-and-rendering-3d-fluids): 3D simulation, corrected advection, and volume rendering.
- [Fedkiw, Stam, Jensen, Visual Simulation of Smoke](https://visionsystems.stanford.edu/papers/smoke/): vorticity confinement.

These are method references. This package does not embed code or assets from the referenced projects.

## Procedural dry-ice billows

Dry Ice combines a connected, ridged low-mist recovery target with a procedural billow field. Jittered cell features derive from seed and simulation time without CPU lifetime instances. Each cell has a cycle duration and phase; cycle-dependent seeds vary radius, position and occupancy. One leaning crown and feathered skirt rise, widen and lower with overlapping growth and settling. Body density is zero at cycle boundaries. Wake activity reduces the body locally; it is not an independently advected, colliding bubble.

Release is the difference of a cumulative release curve over each solver step, including the previous cycle when a step crosses a boundary. A compact horizontal footprint and vertical kernel distribute release, with a separate outward force. Finite support, bounds and obstacles can discard release: body and mist are artistically coupled, not a mass-conserving phase transition.

One compute pass builds current body density and an RGBAHalf source volume containing integrated density release, horizontal force and wake activity. Fluid passes sample the source cache; Planar2D sums release through the column. Two RFloat body volumes allow rendering to interpolate between solver ticks. Existing lighting and volumetric rendering composite the bodies with the transported mist. Switching away from Dry Ice releases these three volumes without clearing the underlying fluid. Reset restores the seeded, staggered field.
