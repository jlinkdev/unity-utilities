# Windowsill

Open **Windowsill.unity**. Select **Windowsill · select for controls** in the Hierarchy and keep the Game view visible while you use its Inspector. Press **Play** to use the same controls on screen. The left pot starts with a mature pothos. The right pot is ready for a cutting.

## Grow a jade shoot

Use **Selected pot** in the Inspector or at the top right of the Game view to frame the active plant. The view follows the shoot as it grows and fits a detached spider plantlet in pot 2. Switch pots to inspect the other specimen. **Both pots** restores the full windowsill. Starting the side-by-side comparison also frames both pots.

Choose **Grow → Jade shoot** in the active pot. Each node carries two leaves. With the default manual moisture, the preset starts with four nodes. Choose **Grow +6** to open more pairs, then **Grow +6** again to see a side branch below the tip. Use **Step +1** for a closer look at a new pair opening. Choose **Simulate plant care** to try the jade's slower drying rate.

## Grow a spider or rubber plant

Select **Pot 1 → Grow → Spider rosette**. The preset starts with mature plantlets at the ends of its runners. Choose **Choose a plantlet** and use **Previous** or **Next** to move the gold ring. **Detach and plant in pot 2** moves that six-leaf rosette into the nursery and selects pot 2. Choose **Grow +6** to watch it fill out. Switch back to pot 1 to inspect the runner where it grew. An occupied nursery offers an explicit **Empty pot 2 for plantlet** action.

Choose **Rubber tree** in the active pot for a deep-green rubber plant. **Variegated preset** switches to the cream-edged form. Under **New planting**, choose Rubber plant, set a seed and a lower **Starting growth**, and enable **Variegated rubber leaves** if desired. Choose **Plant this seed** and step through the unfolding leaves.

### Optional plant care

Each pot starts with care off and a manual **Moisture** slider. Enable **Simulate plant care** for the selected pot, advance time, and watch the soil percentage and leaves. **Water pot** refills the soil and lifts wilted leaves. The spider plant dries and wilts sooner than the rubber plant. Mature specimens still respond. Switch care off at any time to return to manual moisture. **Save windowsill** preserves the setting and soil level in each pot.

## Train a vine

For the side-by-side test, open **Compare**, choose **Start side-by-side**, then **Grow +6**. Pot 1 starts with its growing bud near the pole. Pot 2 has the same pothos cutting in a trailing pose. Both receive the same moisture, light and elapsed time. Select a pot to inspect its growth. **Save windowsill** and **Restore** keep both plants and their shared comparison. **Return to original windowsill** brings back your previous plants and any pending cutting.

Enable **Show growing tip and contacts** to mark the next growing bud in gold. The short dashed line forecasts the next stem segment. Cyan marks stems that met a rim, shelf or pole at birth. **Vine** selects a particular shoot on a multi-shoot plant. Zero follows the next bud anywhere on the specimen. The readout reports the predicted contact and horizontal distance to the pole surface.

Under **Grow**, select **Pot 1**. Move **Pot to front edge (m)**, then choose **Plant this seed** to compare the same pothos at different positions. Step time to watch new internodes clear the rim, follow the sill and drop past its edge. Toggle **Moss pole** to give nearby growing tips a climbing support. Changing supports preserves established stems. In Play mode, drag the plant view to orbit, scroll to zoom, and choose **Fit view** to reset the camera.

## Prune and propagate

Under **Cuttings**, use **Previous**, **Next** or the slider to select a node. The orange ring marks the cut and the highlighted stems show the branch you will remove. Choose **Cut highlighted branch**, then **Plant cutting in pot 2**. Advance pot 2, switch to pot 1, then advance the parent to see its retained bud grow. One cutting can wait on the bench. If pot 2 is occupied, **Empty pot 2 for cutting** clears it.

After cutting a branch, the unoccupied second pot shows a live planting preview. Choose **Let it trail** or **Train to pole**, then adjust **Rotate**, **Tilt** and the pole's two position sliders. The preview starts from the original cutting with each adjustment. The gold bud and readout tell you whether the next segment can reach the support. Choose **Plant cutting in pot 2** to commit that pose. **Compare this cutting in both pots** opens the two presets using your pending specimen.

## Grow a Monstera

Under **Grow**, choose **Climbing Monstera** to place a seeded climbing specimen and its moss pole in the active pot. For the full progression, choose Monstera, set **Starting growth** to zero and choose **Plant this seed**. Use **Grow +6** or the Play clock. Later nodes produce larger leaves with open edge splits and enclosed holes. Aerial roots emerge below the petioles.

To propagate Monstera, grow the **Climbing Monstera** preset, then select **Cuttings**. The selector includes non-basal nodes at depth two or greater whose leaf has matured for five biological growth units. Each has an aerial root. Cut the last viable node for a tip cutting, or choose an earlier node to retain its descendants. Plant it and advance to see a fresh shoot from the rooted node. Advance the donor to see regrowth below the cut.

## Compare six seeds

Open **Tray**. Each position has its own seed and shares the source plant’s recipe settings, age and growing conditions. All six are shown at the same scale. Select by seed or by the numbered marker. **Pin** preserves that specimen across **Reroll unpinned seeds**. **Living copy** and **Static copy** place the selection in pot 2, replacing its occupant. In the editor, **Living prefab…** saves a resumable plant and **Static prefab…** saves a renderer and a UV mesh asset in your project.

**Save windowsill** stores both pots, support settings, the pending cutting, and the tray with its pins in a local save slot. **Restore** returns to the saved setup and view. In Edit mode, saving the Unity scene also preserves the authored specimens.
